Generated by `pnpm lab` (lab/balance.lab.ts). WORLD WEATHER #272–#285 (14 days) × 3 routes × 7 aircraft × 8 pilots = 2352 full headless flights on the real flight, weather, scoring and meta modules, stepped exactly like game/session.ts. Flights are cut at 1.5 × the scoring reference time (straight distance / cruise speed). Wall time 68 s (744 s of flying across parallel shards).

## Findings

### Wave 3: current tuning (balance-loop.md #1–25)

The promise holds on the real models. Surfer vs direct: −16.5 % time and −18 % fuel (median, all tiers), faster in 91 % of pairs, cheaper in 96 %. On boards normalised per aircraft (1000 = the aircraft flown well without reading the sky), the best weather-aware pilot beats the best blind one on speed in 83 % of moderate+extreme pairs (+12.7 %), on eco in 96 % (+32.5 %), on smooth in 97 % of extreme pairs. Surfer vs direct on the speed board: +20 % (median, moderate+extreme).

What does it, by module:
- weather: a low jet (core 4–5.5 km, 2.2× speed) runs 8–12 km beside the straight line with little meander, so riding it is a sideways decision that doesn't cost a 150 km climb. Moderate routes (from severity 0.35) get a storm beside the line, extreme ones a core on it; calm stays clear.
- flight: fuel flow follows delivered thrust (throttle · lapse). Tanks are sized so a direct flight lands with ~17 %. Turbulence fatigue grows with airspeed, so every airframe pays about the same for a core. maxSpeed/cruise is ≈ 1.14 everywhere. The turboprop cruises at 205 m/s and the supersonic at 265 m/s, so their flights last 6–12 minutes.
- scoring: each board is referenced on the aircraft's analytic blind flight (full throttle straight line, best-range straight line at its best altitude, trimmed direct in the route's background air). The weather's gain is put on a shared footing (speed ratio^((V_full/222)^1.05), eco 1000·(1 + (V_range/222)^0.55 · ln ratio), smooth relative to the airframe's own reference). Eco is log-scaled with a timetable. Comfort ignores steady g (5 s high-pass). Weather utilization now reads direct 5 %, surfer 32 %, eco 53 %.
- meta: routes 95–145 km. Aircraft unlocks: narrowbody smooth 800, widebody speed 1050, cargo eco 1100, supersonic speed 1150, experimental smooth 1050 / speed 1150 / eco 1200. Upgrade score unlocks sit at 1050–1500. Blind players stall after the narrowbody, a surfer-only player after the widebody, a player who picks the style per board unlocks everything by flight 20. Upgrades come about one per flight.
- bot (weaknesses fixed, not tuning): the planner evaluates every jet, and jet riders leave the jet in time to line up for the gate.

Per-route board leaders: smooth narrowbody 45 %, speed supersonic 48 %, eco supersonic 48 % (was 100/100/74 %).

Still open:
- The experimental takes 13.8–19.7 minutes on a direct flight. Making it fast (≥ 200 m/s) breaks flight.test's "fixture updraft lifts the experimental ≥ 500 m" (434 m at 200 m/s): the glider identity and the 6–12 min rule can't both hold.
- A direct pass through an extreme core costs a median 8.6 % stress (max 23 %; turboprop/experimental 11–12 %, widebody/cargo 4–5 %). flight.test caps the turboprop at < 60 % after 60 s in uniform severe air, which caps the rate; low-gust airframes stay under 10 %.
- 3 % of arrivals keep > 60 % fuel: eco flights that read the sky well.
- Riding the jet costs comfort in its shear layers, so on calm days the smooth board still favours flying straight.

### Verdict

The promise holds for one aircraft and one situation. It does not hold for the game as tuned at the end of wave 2.

- Time: reading the weather never makes a flight faster. Surfer vs direct is +0.8 % time (median), faster in 16 % of pairs. Surfer-fast vs reckless (same full throttle, weather-aware vs blind) is +0.2 %. On the speed board the best weather-aware pilot is ahead in 31 % of moderate+extreme pairs, median −0.8 %.
- Fuel: surfer saves 1.8 % vs direct. The big eco savings come from flying slow: eco-naive, blind to the weather, gets −31 % vs direct; reading the weather adds −2.5 % on top (eco vs eco-naive).
- The exception is the glider: eco in the experimental burns 28 % less than eco-naive, median eco score 5,593 on extreme days.
- Storms are where skill pays, and the new weather makes that systematic: every extreme route now has a core on the line. Direct takes 21.5 s of severe turbulence on average there; dodging cuts it to 0.4 s and doubles the smooth score (254 → 519) for +0.7 % time.
- Riding and circling in lift costs comfort (surfer vs surfer-smooth −2.5 points for −0.3 % fuel), mostly through the steady-acceleration term of the comfort formula.

### Why jets and lift don't pay (Appendix C)

It's a budget problem, not a piloting one. Climbing from 3 to 10.5 km takes 13–14 minutes and 155–165 km of ground for the regional, narrowbody and widebody, 28 minutes and 210 km for the experimental, 32 minutes and 409 km for the cargo; the turboprop can't get there. The routes are 80–160 km. Coming back down into a gate window that tops out at 6–7 km takes another 42–94 km at idle. So for six aircraft the jet leg has zero length on every route the game generates; the planner's break-even route length is 250–400 km. Only the supersonic (3 minutes, 72 km of climb) can use it, and it still needs a 160 km route to break even on time, at +13 % fuel.

Updrafts can't bridge that: a 13–26 m/s column costs zero progress while circling, so 7 km of climb is 5–9 minutes standing still, more than any route's slack against a direct flight. Crossing lift without circling gives a few hundred metres, which for a 72 t airliner is under 10 s of engine work. The air is a rounding error next to the engines for everything but the glider: weather utilization p50 is 1–6 % on powered aircraft.

What the bot doesn't do (speed-to-fly, multi-column planning, wide lateral routing) is worth a few percent for the glider. It can't turn a 155 km climb into an 80 km route.

### Scoring and progression

- One aircraft owns each board: smooth → widebody 100 % of day×route pairs, speed → experimental 100 %, eco → experimental 74 %. Speed is scored against each aircraft's own cruise speed, so the highest maxSpeed/cruise ratio wins; the supersonic is not the speed aircraft and the cargo is not the eco one.
- The speed board has almost no spread (reckless cargo p25–p90 1,128–1,171): it measures throttle.
- Unlock score thresholds never bind: every simulated player unlocks on flight count alone (2/4/7/10/14/20).
- Fuel and structure never threaten a flight: direct lands with 57 % of the tank (median), 46 % of arrivals have more than 60 % left, zero fuel failures, peak stress ~10 %.

### Recommended changes

Wave 3 (balance loop, see balance-loop.md) acts on these directly: bring the jet within reach of the routes (altitude, start/gate altitudes), make the cheap way up real, re-base scoring references, tighten fuel, re-tune unlocks.
