# Balance lab: does the weather pay?

Generated by `pnpm lab` (lab/balance.lab.ts). WORLD WEATHER #272–#285 (14 days) × 3 routes × 7 aircraft × 8 pilots = 2352 full headless flights on the real flight, weather, scoring and meta modules, stepped exactly like game/session.ts. Flights are cut at 1.5 × the scoring reference time (straight distance / cruise speed). Wall time 37 s (381 s of flying across parallel shards).

Pilots (all share the same human-rate hands: bounded stick rates, ±0.1 g planned manoeuvres, ±0.2 g corrections; reckless ±0.6 g):

| pilot | skill side | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| direct | naive | straight line, gate-window altitude, trimmed cruise |
| reckless | naive | straight line, full throttle, hard hands |
| surfer | weather-aware | altitude plan + lift riding/circling + storm/sink avoidance, cruise speed |
| eco | weather-aware | best-range speed, circles lift to bank energy, idle glides |
| eco-naive | naive | eco speed policy (best range, idle glide to the gate) on the straight line, blind to the weather |
| surfer-smooth | weather-aware | surfer without circling or lift riding: plan + avoidance only |
| surfer-fast | weather-aware | full throttle + altitude plan + avoidance (the weather-aware reckless) |
| surfer-jet | weather-aware | surfer forced to climb to the jet core (or its ceiling) whatever the plan says |

## Findings

### Wave 3: current tuning (balance-loop.md #1–13)

The promise now holds on the real models. Surfer vs direct: −16 % time and −15 % fuel (median, all tiers), faster in 90 % of pairs, cheaper in 93 %. The best weather-aware pilot beats the best naive one on the speed board in 84 % of moderate+extreme pairs (median +10.5 %, calm 96 %) and on the eco board in 91 % (+28 %). Eco vs eco-naive: −26 % fuel and −12 % time, so reading the sky beats flying slow now that the eco board keeps a timetable.

What changed it, in order of effect:
1. A low jet (core 4–5.5 km, 2.2× the old speed) runs 9–13 km beside the straight line with little meander. Finding it is a lateral decision the straight-line pilot never makes, and it sits in the band the routes already fly, so there is no 150 km climb to pay. The planner fix that came with it (evaluate every jet, not the nearest one) was a bot weakness: before it the surfer ignored this jet.
2. Scoring shares boards across aircraft fairly: speed and eco ratios are raised to (cruise/222)^0.7 and (cruise/222)^1.2, smooth rates discomfort against the airframe's ride (gust·cabin/0.6)^0.8, and every airframe has maxSpeed/cruise ≈ 1.14. Per-route board leaders: smooth regional 48 %, speed experimental 50 %, eco supersonic 45 % (was 100/100/74 %).
3. Eco timetable: full score up to 1.25 × reference time, halved every 0.25 × after.
4. Fuel tanks sized so direct lands with 22.5 % (17–30 % by aircraft); reckless empties the tank on 4 extreme flights (and on calm/moderate ones, where it glides in).
5. Weather utilization counts delivered thrust and this airframe's trimmed drag: surfer p50 31 %.

Still open:
- 4 % of arrivals keep more than 60 % fuel: eco flights that ride jets and lift well. That is the eco board working; I'd drop "nobody > 60 %" rather than starve the tanks further.
- Direct flight length: airliners stay in 9–11 min, but the supersonic takes ~5 min and the turboprop/experimental 15–19 min (median). The routes are sized for 222 m/s, so this follows from cruise speeds (420 / 140 / 115 m/s), not from the weather. Fixing it means changing those airframes' identity or per-aircraft route lengths.
- Unlock score thresholds still never bind (every player unlocks on flight count: 2/4/7/10/14/20; an eco-only player never reaches experimental's speed 750). progression.test pins narrowbody's smooth threshold between 200 and 300 and experimental's at 750 ×3, so I left the table alone.
- 24 gate misses, all supersonic (the bot can't turn a 400 m/s aircraft into the gate after a jet leg that ends 10 km off the line).
- Riding the jet costs comfort (surfer −11 comfort points vs direct: shear-layer turbulence), so the smooth board still favours the straight line on calm and moderate days (weather-aware ahead 43 % overall); on extreme days dodging the storm wins 97 % of pairs.

### Verdict

The promise holds for one aircraft and one situation. It does not hold for the game as tuned at the end of wave 2.

- Time: reading the weather never makes a flight faster. Surfer vs direct is +0.8 % time (median), faster in 16 % of pairs. Surfer-fast vs reckless (same full throttle, weather-aware vs blind) is +0.2 %. On the speed board the best weather-aware pilot is ahead in 31 % of moderate+extreme pairs, median −0.8 %.
- Fuel: surfer saves 1.8 % vs direct. The big eco savings come from flying slow: eco-naive, blind to the weather, gets −31 % vs direct; reading the weather adds −2.5 % on top (eco vs eco-naive).
- The exception is the glider: eco in the experimental burns 28 % less than eco-naive, median eco score 5,593 on extreme days.
- Storms are where skill pays, and the new weather makes that systematic: every extreme route now has a core on the line. Direct takes 21.5 s of severe turbulence on average there; dodging cuts it to 0.4 s and doubles the smooth score (254 → 519) for +0.7 % time.
- Riding and circling in lift costs comfort (surfer vs surfer-smooth −2.5 points for −0.3 % fuel), mostly through the steady-acceleration term of the comfort formula.

### Why jets and lift don't pay (Appendix C)

It's a budget problem, not a piloting one. Climbing from 3 to 10.5 km takes 13–14 minutes and 155–165 km of ground for the regional, narrowbody and widebody, 28 minutes and 210 km for the experimental, 32 minutes and 409 km for the cargo; the turboprop can't get there. The routes are 80–160 km. Coming back down into a gate window that tops out at 6–7 km takes another 42–94 km at idle. So for six aircraft the jet leg has zero length on every route the game generates; the planner's break-even route length is 250–400 km. Only the supersonic (3 minutes, 72 km of climb) can use it, and it still needs a 160 km route to break even on time, at +13 % fuel.

Updrafts can't bridge that: a 13–26 m/s column costs zero progress while circling, so 7 km of climb is 5–9 minutes standing still, more than any route's slack against a direct flight. Crossing lift without circling gives a few hundred metres, which for a 72 t airliner is under 10 s of engine work. The air is a rounding error next to the engines for everything but the glider: weather utilization p50 is 1–6 % on powered aircraft.

What the bot doesn't do (speed-to-fly, multi-column planning, wide lateral routing) is worth a few percent for the glider. It can't turn a 155 km climb into an 80 km route.

### Scoring and progression

- One aircraft owns each board: smooth → widebody 100 % of day×route pairs, speed → experimental 100 %, eco → experimental 74 %. Speed is scored against each aircraft's own cruise speed, so the highest maxSpeed/cruise ratio wins; the supersonic is not the speed aircraft and the cargo is not the eco one.
- The speed board has almost no spread (reckless cargo p25–p90 1,128–1,171): it measures throttle.
- Unlock score thresholds never bind: every simulated player unlocks on flight count alone (2/4/7/10/14/20).
- Fuel and structure never threaten a flight: direct lands with 57 % of the tank (median), 46 % of arrivals have more than 60 % left, zero fuel failures, peak stress ~10 %.

### Recommended changes

Wave 3 (balance loop, see balance-loop.md) acts on these directly: bring the jet within reach of the routes (altitude, start/gate altitudes), make the cheap way up real, re-base scoring references, tighten fuel, re-tune unlocks.
