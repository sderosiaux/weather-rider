# Balance lab: does the weather pay?

Generated by `pnpm lab` (lab/balance.lab.ts). WORLD WEATHER #272–#285 (14 days) × 3 routes × 7 aircraft × 8 pilots = 2352 full headless flights on the real flight, weather, scoring and meta modules, stepped exactly like game/session.ts. Flights are cut at 1.5 × the scoring reference time (straight distance / cruise speed). Wall time 323 s.

Pilots (all share the same human-rate hands: bounded stick rates, ±0.1 g planned manoeuvres, ±0.2 g corrections; reckless ±0.6 g):

| pilot | skill side | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| direct | naive | straight line, gate-window altitude, trimmed cruise |
| reckless | naive | straight line, full throttle, hard hands |
| surfer | weather-aware | altitude plan + lift riding/circling + storm/sink avoidance, cruise speed |
| eco | weather-aware | best-range speed, circles lift to bank energy, idle glides |
| eco-naive | naive | eco speed policy (best range, idle glide to the gate) on the straight line, blind to the weather |
| surfer-smooth | weather-aware | surfer without circling or lift riding: plan + avoidance only |
| surfer-fast | weather-aware | full throttle + altitude plan + avoidance (the weather-aware reckless) |
| surfer-jet | weather-aware | surfer forced to climb to the jet core (or its ceiling) whatever the plan says |

## Findings

Read these with the tables below. Every number comes from this run (14 days, 2,352 flights).

### Verdict

The promise holds for one aircraft and one situation. It does not hold for the game as tuned today.

- Time: reading the weather never makes a flight faster. Surfer vs direct is +0.5 % time (median, all tiers), faster in only 21 % of pairs. Surfer-fast vs reckless (same full throttle, weather-aware vs blind) is +0.1 %. On the speed board the best weather-aware pilot trails the best naive one by 0.1 %. Throttle and aircraft decide that board.
- Fuel: surfer saves 1.4 % vs direct. The big eco savings (−32 % vs direct) come from flying slow: eco-naive, blind to the weather, gets −31 %. Reading the weather adds −1.2 % on top (eco vs eco-naive), −6.5 % on extreme days.
- The exception is the glider. Eco in the experimental burns 27.5 % less than eco-naive in the same aircraft, and on extreme days its median eco score is 5,368 (fuel used = 20 % of reference). Only there does the air do most of the work.
- Storms are where skill pays today. The straight line clips a storm core on 43 % of extreme routes and 21 % of moderate ones (0 % calm). On the 42 extreme flights where direct took severe turbulence (16.7 s on average), dodging cuts it to 0.8 s and lifts the smooth score from 297 to 507 (+71 %) for +0.7 % time.
- Riding and circling in lift costs comfort. Surfer vs surfer-smooth (same pilot minus lift riding and circling): −2.5 comfort points and −25 smooth for −0.2 % fuel. Eco vs eco-naive: −12 comfort. The loss comes from the steady-acceleration term of the comfort formula (mean aRms/ACCEL_REF 0.465 for eco vs 0.287 for eco-naive, 4 days sampled), much less from jerk (0.261 vs 0.220).

### Is it the bot?

I checked the obvious bot weaknesses before blaming the models.

- Jets. The surfer's pre-flight planner (bot/planner.ts, built only on trimLevel and the sampled wind) picks the jet in 0 % of flights for six aircraft and 2 % for the supersonic. Forcing it (surfer-jet) costs +1.7 % time, +3.7 % fuel and −10.7 comfort vs direct. Engines climb 2.0–2.6 m/s at 10 km (narrowbody, regional, Appendix A); cargo and turboprop can't hold cruise speed up there at all. Then a 6–8 km descent into a gate window that tops out at 7 km eats 60–100 km of an 80–160 km route.
- Control quality. The inner loops pass unit tests on all seven airframes (90° turns, 1,000 m climbs with < 150 m overshoot and |Δg| < 0.25, orbit capture, updraft circling). The headless runner reproduces game/session.ts exactly (test in bot/autopilot.test.ts). 2,350 of 2,352 flights arrive (2 eco misses in the supersonic).
- What the bot doesn't do: speed-to-fly in sink and lift (MacCready), lateral routing for tailwind beyond ±50° local dodges, planning across several lift columns. For gliders that is worth a few percent. It can't close the gap to a jet that sits out of reach.

For anything but the glider, the atmosphere is small next to the engines: weather utilization p50 is 1–6 % for every powered aircraft (experimental p90: 39).

### Scoring and progression

- One aircraft owns each board. Over the 42 day×route pairs, the best smooth score always comes from the widebody, the best speed score always from the experimental, the best eco score from the experimental (71 %) or the supersonic (29 %). Speed is scored against each aircraft's own cruise speed, so the winner is whoever has the highest maxSpeed/cruise ratio and the largest tailwind share (experimental 150/115, turboprop 175/140). The supersonic is not the speed aircraft (median best 1,248 vs 1,385), and the cargo is not the eco aircraft (1,099, lowest of all).
- The speed board has almost no spread: reckless in the cargo scores 1,128–1,171 from p25 to p90. There is nothing to master.
- References are off: direct at trimmed cruise scores speed ~1,077 and eco ~820–880, not 1,000, because REF_THROTTLE 0.6 sits below real trim throttle (0.62–0.93, Appendix A). Eco reaches 9,775 in the glider (cap 10,000), so the boards don't share a scale.
- Unlock score thresholds never bind. Every simulated player (direct, reckless, surfer, eco, board-savvy) unlocks each aircraft on its flight count alone: 2, 4, 7, 10, 14, 20. Even p10 scores clear 250/450/500/600, and since progress.best is global, widebody smooth scores clear the experimental's 750.
- Fuel and structure never threaten a flight: zero fuel failures, zero overstress. Direct lands with 42–72 % of the tank (median by aircraft), reckless with 27–70 %. Peak stress seen is 10 % (direct through an extreme storm). SPEC §7's "Fuel remaining: 6 %" can't happen.

### Recommended changes, ranked

1. **weather** · `layout.ts` jets, `y0 = min(ceiling − 1500, 9900 + 1300·rj())`. Evidence: surfer plans the jet in 0–2 % of flights; forcing it costs +1.7 % time / +3.7 % fuel. Proposal: cores at 5.5–8 km (or push `rv` down so the lower third starts near 5 km), reachable by every airframe but the turboprop in the first quarter of a route. Pass criterion for the next `pnpm lab`: surfer-jet ≤ −5 % time vs direct.
2. **flight** · `model.ts` fuel burn `fuelBurnFullKgS · throttle` ignores thrust lapse, so range per kg falls with altitude (turboprop 0.055 → 0.045 km/kg from 2 to 6 km, experimental 0.232 → 0.185 from 2 to 10 km, jets −7 to −11 %). Climbing to find wind is taxed twice. Proposal: burn ∝ thrust (`throttle · thrustLapse(h)`), so altitude is at worst fuel-neutral. Only matters once #1 lands; re-tune tanks after (#6).
3. **scoring** · speed and eco references. `T_ref` from each aircraft's own cruise makes the experimental win 100 % of speed boards. Proposal: one route reference speed for all aircraft (meta's `REF_GAME_CRUISE_MS` = 222 m/s), so the supersonic becomes the speed aircraft that meta's unlock table assumes; `F_ref` from `trimLevel` at 3 km cruise instead of `REF_THROTTLE` 0.6, so direct ≈ 1,000; cap or log-scale eco (9,775 seen).
4. **scoring** · comfort. The `aRms / ACCEL_REF` term charges steady g (orbits, gentle climbs in lift) like bumps, and it carries most of the comfort cost of using lift (eco 0.465 vs 0.287; jerk 0.261 vs 0.220). Proposal: aRms on high-passed cabin accel (subtract a ~5 s moving average), or `ACCEL_REF` 2 → 4 m/s². Keep jerk as is: it is the turbulence signal, and it already separates dodgers from direct.
5. **meta** · `AIRCRAFT_UNLOCKS`: thresholds never bind. After #3, re-run the lab and set each threshold near the median of the aircraft the player flies while chasing it. From today's numbers: narrowbody smooth 700 (regional p50 671–695), widebody smooth 780 (narrowbody 731–753), cargo eco 1,150 (widebody eco p50 1,113), supersonic speed 1,140 (cargo reckless 1,145), experimental smooth 850 / speed 1,240 / eco 1,600. Alternative: track `best` per aircraft instead of globally.
6. **flight** · `aircraft.ts` `fuelCapacityKg`: direct lands with 42–72 %, zero fuel failures in 2,352 flights. Proposal: ×0.55–0.6, so direct lands with ~15–25 % and reckless on a long route can run dry. Design call: it turns fuel from a score into a constraint.
7. **flight** · `model.ts` `TURBULENCE_STRESS_RATE` 0.0006/s: stress peaks at 10 %, so SPEC §4's stress column is invisible. If storm cores should threaten the airframe, ~×10 (16 s of severe turbulence would then cost ~10–15 %).
8. **weather** · storms on the line: dodging is the one skill that pays, and calm routes never offer it (0 % of calm flights clip a core, 21 % moderate). Proposal: from severity 0.3, guarantee one storm or strong sink within ±3 km of the straight line, so every daily route has a dodge decision.
9. **scoring** · weather utilization reads 1–6 % on every powered flight; as a results stat it tells the player nothing. Scale it against the route's straight-line engine energy, or hide it until #1/#2 give the air a bigger share.

Order: 1 and 2 decide whether the weather can pay at all, 3 and 4 whether the boards see it, 5 needs a re-run after 3.
